About

I study how knowledge work changes when AI joins the team — how people come to trust, doubt, and argue with algorithmic systems.

I'm a PhD candidate in Management (Information Systems) at the Weatherhead School of Management, Case Western Reserve University, graduating by May 2027. My dissertation is a 25-month ethnography of radiologists working alongside FDA-cleared AI models — tracing how epistemic trust forms, why doubt does useful work, and how much invisible labor goes into making AI outputs usable in everyday practice.

Alongside this, I study algorithmic management — power, precarity, and loneliness in platform work — and health data infrastructures, as part of the NIH/NCI-funded team building PopCASE, a population cancer data platform.

Before the PhD, I trained as a mechanical engineer and in nonprofit management. In 2013 I co-founded Sahridhaya, a youth-focused nonprofit in Kerala, and later worked with Kerala Startup Mission and the International Centre for Free and Open-Source Software on innovation programs and assistive technology.

Marginalia
A local-first reading companion for academic research

A Linux desktop app that connects your Zotero library to a language model running on your own machine. It brings back one paper a day from your own library and prepares you to read it: questions to carry into the full text, your own notes on it, and related papers you already have. It asks questions instead of writing summaries, so the reading is still yours.

2026 · Python
dcog-lexicon
Mapping how cognition is shared between people and AI

A codebook and local AI tool that turns ethnographic fieldnotes into activity maps of human–AI work: who or what did each step, on which screen, and how each claim was checked, accepted or left open. The model runs on the researcher's own machine, because fieldnotes from hospitals and other sensitive sites can't go to a cloud service. It drafts a map; the researcher checks and corrects it.

2026 · JavaScript & Python
DreamON
A quiet room for deep work on GNOME

A top-bar extension that keeps only one practice's apps on screen for a sitting: reading in Zotero and Obsidian, or writing and reviewing in Google Docs. Other windows, notifications and off-task browser tabs are set aside until the bell. A companion app shows what the practice adds up to: sittings, pages read, streaks and the distractions that pulled at you.

Assistive technology · ICFOSS · Researcher & Project Manager

2018 · Hardware & firmware
T-Slide Mouse
Kerala's first customizable ergonomic mouse

An open-source USB mouse for persons with disabilities, operated with a thumb-slide joystick and two touch sensors, with optional vibration for haptic feedback. It runs on an Arduino Pro Micro and registers as a standard USB input device, so it works on any computer without drivers. The case is 3D printed, and the firmware is calibrated to each user.
